How to Reduce Screen Time and Build a Healthier Digital Routine

Screens are part of work, learning, relationships and entertainment. The aim is not necessarily to avoid technology, but to notice when use feels automatic or gets in the way of sleep, focus, movement or relationships.

Updated 2026-11-24 · General educational information · Not clinically reviewed

Start by noticing, not judging

Check your device's screen-time report and identify the apps or times that account for most recreational use. Separate necessary work or study from time you would like to change.

1. Choose one clear target

Instead of trying to cut all screen use, choose one pattern: scrolling in bed, checking notifications during meals, or opening an app whenever you feel bored. Set a small and measurable goal for that pattern.

2. Add friction to automatic checking

  • Turn off non-essential notifications and badges.
  • Move high-distraction apps off your home screen or sign out after use.
  • Set app limits or focus modes, then review whether they help.
  • Keep your phone out of reach during focused work, meals or conversations.

3. Protect sleep and downtime

Try a consistent wind-down routine and put screens aside before bed if they keep you alert or delay sleep. Charge your phone away from your bed if that is practical. If your work requires evening screen use, reduce stimulating recreational use and create a calmer transition to sleep.

4. Replace, don't just remove

Decide what you will do instead: read a few pages, stretch, walk, cook, talk with someone, listen to music or work on a hobby. Keep the alternative easy to start.

5. Review weekly

Look at your screen-time report once a week. Ask whether your changes improved concentration, sleep, mood or time for other activities. Adjust the plan without treating one high-use day as failure.

6. Get extra support if needed

If screen or gaming use feels persistently uncontrollable or is causing significant problems with daily functioning, consider discussing it with a qualified health professional. For general habit support, see How to Build Healthy Habits.

Important: There is no single screen-time limit that fits every adult. Consider the purpose, timing and impact of your screen use, not only the total number of hours.
