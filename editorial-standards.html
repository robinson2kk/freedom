Editorial standards & professional review

FREEDOM publishes general educational resources about habits, behaviour change and recovery. We aim to make information clear, respectful, evidence-aware and honest about its limitations.

Current status: FREEDOM is building its professional review network. Unless an individual page explicitly names a qualified reviewer and review date, do not assume that page has received clinical review. We will never label content “medically reviewed” simply because it was written with AI assistance or checked by a general editor.

How content is developed

1. Topic and risk assessment

We identify the reader's question, intended audience and potential safety risks. Topics involving diagnosis, medicines, withdrawal, emergencies or treatment require particular caution.

2. Research and sourcing

Writers prioritise current government and public-health guidance, clinical guidelines, professional organisations, systematic reviews and peer-reviewed research. Important claims should link to sources.

3. Drafting and plain-language edit

Content is structured for clarity, non-judgmental language and practical understanding. Evidence, uncertainty, personal experience and opinion must not be presented as interchangeable.

4. Appropriate expert review

Clinical claims and safety-sensitive material should be reviewed by a suitably qualified professional with relevant subject expertise, separate from the writer where feasible.

5. Editorial checks

We check readability, links, references, dates, scope, privacy and whether the page clearly explains when professional or emergency help is needed.

6. Publication and maintenance

Pages should display a publication date and, where reviewed, the named reviewer's credentials and review date. We correct errors and update content when evidence or guidance changes.

How review labels work

Editorially checked

Reviewed for clarity, structure, spelling, links and consistency by the FREEDOM editorial process. This label does not mean clinical approval.

Expert reviewed

Reviewed by a named contributor for subject-matter accuracy. The reviewer's role, relevant qualifications, scope of review and date should be disclosed.

Clinically reviewed

Use only when a suitably qualified healthcare professional has reviewed the clinical content and safety implications within their competence. Display the reviewer's name, verified credentials or registration where appropriate, specialty and review date. Clinical review is not a guarantee of outcomes or a substitute for individual care.

Corrections and updates

Readers and professionals can report an error, outdated reference, safety concern or unclear statement through the feedback page. We will assess reports, correct material errors where appropriate and update the page's revision date. For urgent safety concerns, use the Immediate Help page rather than waiting for an editorial response.

Professional contributors

We welcome qualified psychologists, psychiatrists, counsellors, doctors, addiction specialists and other relevant professionals to propose topics, review drafts, contribute educational material or provide feedback. Contributions are voluntary unless a separate written agreement says otherwise. Submission does not create an employment relationship, endorsement or guarantee of publication.

  • We ask contributors to disclose qualifications, relevant registration and areas of expertise.
  • Reviewers should only review topics within their competence and identify conflicts of interest.
  • Do not submit patient/client records, identifiable case histories or confidential workplace material.
  • FREEDOM may edit for clarity and length, but substantive clinical changes should be returned to the reviewer for approval before a clinical-review label is used.
  • Contributors retain rights in their original work unless a separate written licence or agreement is agreed.

Ownership and independence

FREEDOM is an independent educational project. We aim to disclose relevant sponsorships, commercial relationships and conflicts of interest when they affect content. Product recommendations or promotional material must not be presented as independent clinical advice.

This policy describes the intended editorial process. It does not claim that FREEDOM currently has an established clinical editorial board or that every existing page has been clinically reviewed.
